## Programme Objectives

By the end of the programme, students will be able to:

- Identify common cyber threats (phishing, brute-force attacks, malware, fake downloads, and social engineering).
- Explain core concepts of passwords, encryption, authentication, permissions, and network safety.
- Build, test, and evaluate crack-resistant passphrases using password entropy simulators.
- Inspect and evaluate URLs to detect phishing scams and spoofed login pages.
- Understand how data encryption protects personal information on public networks.
- Spot malware traps, fake game currency scams (e.g., Robux/V-Bucks generators), and rogue browser extensions.
- Manage Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and audit device permissions.
- Practice multi-factor authentication (MFA) and master secure Chromebook session locking and logout procedures.
- Understand legal and ethical boundaries under Pakistan's Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA 2016) and know official reporting avenues (NCCIA Helpline 1799, Police Helpline 15).

# Lesson 1: Account Defense, Passwords & Social Engineering

**Duration:** 2 Hours

### Topics Covered

- Introduction to cybersecurity, ethical defense, and digital assets.
- Password entropy: how brute-force algorithms crack simple passwords.
- The Passphrase Formula: combining length, words, numbers, and symbols.
- Social engineering: deception, emotional manipulation, and fake urgency.
- URL anatomy: domain names, HTTPS vs. HTTP, and typosquatting detection.
- Data encryption: plaintext vs. ciphertext and why open Wi-Fi eavesdropping happens.
- Defensive response: Stop, Inspect, Verify, and Report.

---

## Lab 1: The Password Cracker Challenge

**Objective:** Test password strength against brute-force attacks and create a crack-proof passphrase.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Simulator:** [Security.org How Secure Is My Password?](https://www.security.org/how-secure-is-my-password/) *(Client-side only; does not send data over the network)*
- **Interactive Game:** [Google Interland: Tower of Treasure](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/interland/tower-of-treasure)
- Learner Cyber Notebook

### Procedure
1. Students input commonly used weak passwords into the simulator (e.g., `football123`, `princess`, their first name).
2. Observe the simulator's calculated time-to-crack (often *"instantly"* or *"under 3 seconds"*).
3. Introduce the **Passphrase Rule**: select 3–4 unrelated memorable words, insert numbers, and attach special symbols (e.g., `Blue!Giraffe#Eats99Pizzas`).
4. Enter the new passphrase into the simulator and observe the calculation leap to *"millions of years."*
5. Complete the *Tower of Treasure* challenge to practice locking personal digital valuables in virtual vaults.
6. Record the private passphrase formula pattern into the learner notebook without revealing the exact secret words.

### Learning Outcome
Students understand how automated cracking tools operate, why length outperforms short complexity, and how to craft uncrackable passphrases without reusing passwords.

## Lab 2: Phish Hunter & The Fake Link Trap

**Objective:** Detect phishing messages, spoofed domains, and social engineering tricks.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Simulator:** [Google Phishing Quiz](https://phishingquiz.withgoogle.com/)
- **Interactive Game:** [Google Interland: Reality River](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/interland/reality-river)
- Sample Phishing Analysis Flashcards

### Procedure
1. Open the *Google Phishing Quiz* simulator on student Chromebooks or tablets.
2. Examine simulated real-world scenarios: game reward emails, account expiration warnings, and fake Google Classroom password resets.
3. Practice the **Hover Technique**: placing the cursor over links without clicking to reveal the true destination URL in the browser status bar.
4. Identify typosquatting domain indicators (e.g., `accounts.g00gle.com` vs. `accounts.google.com` or `roblox-free-robux.xyz`).
5. Cross the *Reality River* by evaluating questions that test honesty, false urgency, and suspicious file attachments.

### Learning Outcome
Students learn to pause before clicking, inspect destination URLs, spot the red flags of social engineering, and report suspicious messages.

## Lab 3: The Secret Code Breakers

**Objective:** Understand data encryption, ciphers, and how public Wi-Fi networks expose unencrypted information.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Simulator:** [PBS NOVA Cybersecurity Lab - Cryptography Challenge](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/labs/lab/cyber/)
- **Alternate Cipher Tool:** [CryptoClub Caesar & Substitution Cipher Tools](https://www.cryptoclub.org/)

### Procedure
1. Introduce the concept of plaintext vs. ciphertext through the historical Caesar Shift Cipher.
2. Split students into three roles: Sender, Receiver, and "Snooper on Public Wi-Fi."
3. Send an unencrypted message across the room; demonstrate how the Snooper reads it instantly.
4. Load the *CryptoClub / NOVA Cryptography* simulator. Select a shared key offset ($+3$ or $+7$).
5. Encode a secret message into ciphertext. Transmit it across the simulated channel.
6. The Snooper intercepts only scrambled characters, while the intended receiver uses the private key to decode it.
7. Connect this concept to why HTTPS (the padlock icon) is essential when connecting to home and school Wi-Fi.

### Learning Outcome
Students understand how cryptographic algorithms protect private messages, login credentials, and personal data from network interception.

# Lesson 2: Malware Defense, Network Safety & Device Hardening

**Duration:** 2 Hours

### Topics Covered

- Malware types: Viruses, Trojans, Adware, and Spyware explained in kid-friendly terms.
- The "Free Game" trap: drive-by downloads, fake mod installers, and bogus game currency.
- Chrome Web Store safety: auditing permissions on browser extensions.
- Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and the permanent digital footprint.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA / 2FA) as a dual-key security vault.
- Chromebook security hygiene: screen locking, private browsing limits, and safe logout.
- Incident reporting: school escalations, parent alerts, and NCCIA Helpline 1799.

## Lab 4: Trojan Horse & The Fake Download Minefield

**Objective:** Identify deceptive download buttons, avoid malicious executables, and audit browser extension permissions.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Simulator:** [BBC Bitesize Cyber Security Challenge & Safe Surfing Simulator](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/topics/z67446f)
- **Interactive Defense Game:** [PBS NOVA Cybersecurity Lab - Malware Defense](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/labs/lab/cyber/)
- Interactive "Spot-the-Real-Button" Web Sandbox

### Procedure
1. Display a mock webpage offering a "Free Minecraft Skin / Game Mod" packed with four misleading green "DOWNLOAD NOW" advertisements.
2. Use the inspection checklist to identify the fake banners vs. genuine verified download sources.
3. Access the *PBS NOVA Cybersecurity Lab* network defense level: defend a simulated computer network against incoming worms, virus outbreaks, and Trojan horses.
4. Grade 5 Chromebook extension: Open `chrome://extensions/` on school devices. Review what permissions extensions request (e.g., *"Read and change all data on websites you visit"*).
5. Formulate the **Safe Download Triple Check Rule**:
   - Check the official store/publisher.
   - Check required permissions.
   - Obtain teacher/parent authorization before installing any file or extension.

### Learning Outcome
Students recognize malicious advertising, understand the risks of untrusted downloads, and learn how malware sneaks onto computers disguised as games.

## Lab 5: The OSINT Detective & Privacy Shield

**Objective:** Discover how small pieces of Personally Identifiable Information (PII) reveal personal identity, and learn how to lock down profile privacy.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Simulator:** [Google Interland: Mindful Mountain](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/interland/mindful-mountain)
- Case Study Image Deck: Mock Social Media Photo Investigation

### Procedure
1. Display a sample image of a child posing with a certificate or pet.
2. Act as "Privacy Detectives" and spot hidden PII: school logo on uniform, street name on background signs, house number, geotag data, and birthday banners.
3. Calculate how easily an unknown person can piece together the child's identity, routine, and location.
4. Launch the *Mindful Mountain* simulation: navigate puzzles requiring quick decisions on what information is safe to broadcast and what must remain private.
5. Sort 10 sample data points into two buckets:
   - **Safe to Share:** Favorite cartoon, favorite color, generic gaming nickname.
   - **Never Share Without Parents:** Real full name, school name, home address, phone numbers, location check-ins.

### Learning Outcome
Students understand that the digital footprint is permanent and learn to safeguard Personally Identifiable Information from public exposure.

---

## Lab 6: The Two-Lock Safe Room & Chromebook Lockdown

**Objective:** Implement Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) and master session lockdown on shared devices.

### Online Simulators & Tools
- **Interactive Walkthrough:** [Google Account Safety Center Interactive Demo](https://safety.google/authentication/)
- Classroom 2FA Physical/Digital Simulation Sandbox
- School Chromebooks

### Procedure
1. Explain the **Bank Vault Analogy**: one key (password) is not enough if someone steals it; you need a second separate key (a phone prompt or 6-digit code).
2. Run a live classroom demo: a student volunteer enters a compromised password on the demo screen; the login is halted because the second factor prompts the teacher's device.
3. Students simulate receiving an unexpected security alert: *"Did you just try to sign in from another city?"* and practice triggering the defensive `NO, IT WASN'T ME` protocol.
4. **The Chromebook Fast Lockdown Drill:**
   - Practice the instant lock hotkey (`Search + L`).
   - Clean sign-out workflow: ensuring accounts do not stay active on shared devices.
   - Unchecking "Remember Password" on public or classroom terminals.
5. Fill out the student section of the **Family Device Defense Agreement**.

### Learning Outcome
Students master multi-factor authentication concepts, stop unauthorized session takeovers, and maintain strict physical and digital hygiene on school Chromebooks.

# Safety, Ethics & Acceptable Use Guidelines

- **Defensive Use Only:** All tools and techniques taught in this programme are solely for self-defense, account protection, and awareness.
- **Never Test on Unauthorized Systems:** Students must only input passwords or test security tools within the designated sandbox simulators, never on someone else's account.
- **No Blame Culture:** If a child accidentally clicks an untrusted link or enters credentials on a suspicious site, they must report it to a parent or teacher immediately without fear of reprimand.
- **Adult Permission for Downloads:** Students must never download games, software, or browser extensions without direct adult authorization.
- **Official Reporting Protocol:** For serious digital harassment, account extortion, or identity theft, escalate to school administration or report directly via **NCCIA Helpline 1799** or **Police Helpline 15**.

At the end of the programme, students should be able to:

- Spot at least 3 signs of a phishing email or fake URL before clicking.
- Explain the role of encryption in safeguarding data across open Wi-Fi.
- Identify fake download buttons and reject unverified software/extensions.
- Differentiate between safe general information and sensitive PII.
- Execute proper screen locking and account sign-out on shared Chromebooks.
- Explain what Multi-Factor Authentication is and how it prevents account takeovers.
