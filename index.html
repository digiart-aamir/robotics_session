Core Competencies

What Students Will Learn

A proven progressive pathway covering circuit theory, active electronic components, sensors, and practical troubleshooting.

Component Mastery

Identify breadboards, LEDs, resistors, switches, buzzers, tilt sensors, and DC motors by sight and function.

Circuit Principles

Understand voltage, current, resistance, LED polarity (anode/cathode), and the role of current-limiting resistors.

Sensors & Motion

Harness light sensors (LDRs) and orientation tilt switches to trigger real-world alarms and spin motor fans.

Fault Troubleshooting

Form scientific curiosity by debugging loose connections, reversed polarity, open loops, and loose jumper leads.

6 Hands-on Engineering Projects

Step-by-step builds using child-safe components on a breadboard. Click any image to view in full resolution.

Project 1 • Lesson 1
Time: 25 mins Output: Light

Mini LED Torch

Objective: Build a complete battery-powered handheld torch while mastering LED polarity and resistor protection.
Components Used
2 × AA Batteries Battery Holder 1 × Red/White LED 1 × 330Ω Resistor Jumper Wires Cardboard Housing
Build Procedure
  1. Identify positive (+) and negative (-) battery terminals.
  2. Determine LED anode (long leg) and cathode (short leg).
  3. Wire battery positive to resistor, then to LED anode.
  4. Connect LED cathode back to battery negative to close the circuit.
  5. Mount securely into custom cardboard torch structure.
Learning: Complete circuits, LED polarity & resistor safety.
Project 2 • Lesson 1
Time: 25 mins Output: Controlled Light

Push-Button Light

Objective: Control an LED dynamically using an interactive push-button switch to grasp open vs. closed circuits.
Components Used
Mini Breadboard Push Button Switch 1 × LED 1 × 330Ω Resistor 2 × AA Battery Pack Jumper Wires
Build Procedure
  1. Place the push-button switch across the center divider of the breadboard.
  2. Wire the switch in series with the resistor and LED.
  3. Press the button to complete the loop (closed circuit) and illuminate LED.
  4. Release the button to break the connection (open circuit).
Learning: Understand manual switches & current interruption.
Project 3 • Lesson 1
Time: 25 mins Output: Acoustic Sound (Buzzer)

Electronic Quiz Buzzer

Objective: Convert electrical energy into audible sound waves for a fun, competitive classroom game quiz panel.
Components Used
Active Buzzer (Piezo) Tactile Push Button Battery Supply Jumper Wires Cardboard Quiz Console
Build Procedure
  1. Verify the active buzzer's rated polarity (+ and - leads).
  2. Connect the buzzer in series with the push button and battery.
  3. Press button to produce an instant audible tone.
  4. Decorate and install onto a cardboard quiz contestant console.
Learning: Electrical-to-acoustic energy conversion & signals.
Project 4 • Lesson 2
Time: 25 mins Output: Alarm Sound

Tilt-Activated Alarm

Objective: Engineer an orientation-sensitive security alarm using a mechanical ball-tilt switch sensor.
Components Used
Metallic Tilt Switch Active Buzzer Mini Breadboard Battery Supply Jumper Leads
Build Procedure
  1. Mount the cylindrical tilt switch vertically on the breadboard.
  2. Connect in series with the buzzer and battery power source.
  3. Observe conductibility as internal metallic ball rolls with gravity.
  4. Gently tilt the circuit past 45° to sound the intrusion alarm.
Learning: Sensor triggering, motion detection, and security circuits.
Project 5 • Lesson 2
Time: 30 mins Output: Measured/Visual Light Response

Light Detector

Objective: Discover photo-sensing physics by testing how ambient light changes an LDR's resistance and affects circuit behavior.
Components Used
Light-Dependent Resistor (LDR) 1 × Bright LED 1 × 330Ω Resistor 2 × AA Batteries Mini Breadboard
Build Procedure
  1. Observe LDR sensor surface under direct room lighting.
  2. Build series circuit with LDR, protective resistor, and LED.
  3. Cup hands or shade LDR to observe resistance change.
  4. Observe how light variations regulate electron current.
Learning: Optical sensors, variable resistance & environmental triggers.
Project 6 • Lesson 2
Time: 30 mins Output: Mechanical Rotation & Airflow

Mini Motor Fan

Objective: Convert electrical energy into rotational kinetic motion and aerodynamic airflow using a 3–6V DC motor.
Components Used
Small 3–6V DC Motor Lightweight Propeller Blade Cardboard Stand 2 × AA Battery Pack Jumper Leads
Build Procedure
  1. Inspect motor terminals and verify rated supply voltage.
  2. Affix DC motor securely onto a sturdy cardboard support.
  3. Press-fit the lightweight plastic propeller fan blade onto shaft.
  4. Connect to battery pack and feel the refreshing airflow.
Learning: Electromagnetic rotation, mechanical propulsion & aerodynamics.

Safety Guidelines

  • Use strictly low-voltage safe battery power (3V nominal).
  • Always disconnect power before re-routing breadboard connections.
  • Always use an appropriate current-limiting resistor with each LED.
  • Secure DC motors and fan propellers firmly before applying current.
  • Keep hair and loose items clear from spinning propeller blades.
  • Continuous adult mentoring provided throughout all activities.

Expected Outcomes

  • Draw and explain basic schematic circuit loops and polarities.
  • Explain why current-limiting resistors prevent LED burnout.
  • Demonstrate manual switching on both series and open configurations.
  • Calibrate environmental response using tilt and light sensor circuits.
  • Independently detect and resolve at least one real circuit fault.
