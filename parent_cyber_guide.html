Empowering Safe, Confident Digital Homes

A practical, light-theme handbook for parents of Grades 3–5. Covering the last 3 classroom labs, Google Family Link setups, anti-bullying response protocols, and home illegal content filtering.

💡 The #1 Rule for Parents: Help-Seeking First

National research confirms that over 50% of children who face online bullying tell nobody—chiefly because they fear their phone, tablet, or console will be confiscated. By assuring your child that reporting an issue will never lead to device bans, you establish the open-door trust essential for their safety.

Classroom Immersion

What Your Child Mastered in Labs 4, 5 & 6

In the final hours of the CyberGuard programme, your child practiced 3 critical hands-on defense simulations. Here is how those lessons protect your household:

SIMULATION 04

Trojan Horse & Fake Downloads

Children frequently see tempting popups promising "Free Robux", "Minecraft Mods", or "Game Cheats". Cadets learned that fake download buttons hide adware, spyware, and keyloggers that steal passwords.

Home Protection Rule: Turn off "Automatic Downloads" in Chrome settings and audit installed browser extensions every month.
SIMULATION 05

OSINT Detective & PII Privacy

Cadets analyzed casual photos to spot hidden Personally Identifiable Information (PII): school crests, house numbers, street signs, and birth dates. They practiced redacting details before posting.

The Billboard Test: Ask your child: "Would you put this photo on a public highway billboard?" If not, do not post it online.
SIMULATION 06

Session Lock & Password Care

Cadets learned why passwords should never be shared with friends and mastered rapid screen locking on school Chromebooks using the Search + L keyboard shortcut.

The Friend Rule: Explain that true friends never ask for passwords. Keep account credentials strictly between child and parents.
Device Guardrails

10-Minute Google & Home Safety Setup

You do not need an IT degree to protect your home. Implement these four essential guardrails to shield children from illegal and inappropriate materials:

📱

Google Family Link (Android & Chromebooks)

Free Google Management Suite

Link your child's Google account to your phone to monitor activity, approve app installations, and prevent accidental purchases.

  • 1 Install Google Family Link on your parent phone (Android or iOS).
  • 2 Enable "Require approval for all app downloads" from the Play Store.
  • 3 Set daily bedtime lockouts (e.g., devices automatically lock at 8:30 PM).
🔍

Google SafeSearch & YouTube Kids

Filtering Search & Video Streams

Prevent explicit imagery, adult web pages, and violent content from appearing in casual Google and video searches.

  • 1 Navigate to google.com/safesearch and toggle Filter on.
  • 2 On YouTube, turn on Restricted Mode or switch children under 12 to YouTube Kids.
  • 3 Select the "Explore" (Ages 8–12) content level for safe educational discovery.
🌐

Home Wi-Fi Router DNS Shield (Zero Cost)

Whole-House Automatic Protection

Block adult, illegal, and malicious domains across every device connected to your home Wi-Fi router without installing extra apps.

  • 1 Log into your home Wi-Fi router settings (usually 192.168.1.1).
  • 2 Change Primary DNS to 1.1.1.3 and Secondary to 1.0.0.3 (Cloudflare Family Safe).
  • 3 Restart router: adult content and known phishing URLs are blocked network-wide.
💬

WhatsApp & Multiplayer Gaming Guardrails

Restricting Unsolicited Direct Contact

Most harassment occurs in unmoderated group chats and game voice channels (Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite).

  • 1 In WhatsApp: Settings ➔ Privacy ➔ Groups ➔ Change to "My Contacts Only".
  • 2 In Roblox: Settings ➔ Privacy ➔ Set "Who can chat with me in app" to Friends Only.
  • 3 Mute open public voice chat lobbies so children are not exposed to toxic speech.
Emotional Safeguarding

Spotting & Handling Online Bullying

How to recognize subtle distress signals at home and execute the school-taught behavioral protocol:

🚩 4 Warning Signs to Watch For

  • •
    Sudden Screen Hiding: Quickly tilting devices away or switching tabs whenever a parent approaches the room.
  • •
    Emotional Volatility: Showing visible distress, anger, or tears immediately after receiving notifications.
  • •
    Reluctance for School: Complaining of frequent headaches or stomach aches to avoid attending class or clubs.
  • •
    Withdrawal from Peers: Suddenly quitting games they previously loved or dropping out of class WhatsApp groups.

The 4-Step Student Action Plan

Remind your child of the universal protocol they rehearsed in the classroom:

1. STOP ✋

Take a breath. Do not reply or send angry messages back. Bullies thrive on reactions.

2. BLOCK 🚫

Mute or block the person on the game, WhatsApp, or chat board immediately.

3. SAVE 📸

Take clean screenshots showing usernames and timestamps. Never delete the evidence.

4. TELL 🗣️

Confide in a parent or teacher right away. You are safe and never in trouble for telling.
